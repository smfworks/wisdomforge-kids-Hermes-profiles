## Circle You Control — emerging band — synthetic demo

> Synthetic transcript for WisdomForge Phase 2 demo publication. No real
> child data. Profile: Juniper, emerging band (15–18), sitting:
> Character — The Stoics — circle-you-control.

---

**Juniper:** I have a paper due Friday on whether Stoicism is relevant
today. I think it's basically just "don't worry about things you can't
control" which is kind of obvious.

**Guide:** Before we go further — what have you read on the Stoics so far?

**Juniper:** Skimmed the sitting. Epictetus, the circle thing. I know the
basics.

**Guide:** Good — you've started. One hint: Epictetus didn't say "don't
worry." He said something sharper about *what counts as yours*. Can you
state the distinction in your own words — what is inside the circle and
what is outside it, as Epictetus defines it?

**Juniper:** Inside is like, your choices and your effort. Outside is
everything else — what other people do, what happens to you.

**Guide:** That's the core. Now a harder question: is your reputation
inside or outside the circle?

**Juniper:** Outside. Other people form opinions.

**Guide:** Right. And is the *effort you put into this paper* inside or
outside?

**Juniper:** Inside. That's my work.

**Guide:** Here's where it gets uncomfortable. The emerging-band sitting
asks you to connect prohairesis — the faculty of choice — to academic
integrity and agent permissions. So: if you asked a model to write this
paper and turned it in as your work, where did you move the center?

**Juniper:** I guess... I'd be moving the thing that's mine — my
judgment, my argument — into something outside the circle. The model's
output.

**Juniper:** But wait — that's the whole Seneca problem. You can preach
inner freedom while shipping work you couldn't reproduce.

**Guide:** You just named the gap. The sitting calls that "preaching
inner freedom while shipping work you could not reproduce." Can you say
whether that's a fair criticism of someone who uses AI to write a
philosophy paper and then argues Stoicism is about self-command?

**Juniper:** It's not just unfair — it's the exact contradiction. You're
claiming the center is yours while literally outsourcing the center. The
model is doing your choosing for you.

**Guide:** That's the argument. Now: what would a *Stoic-aligned* use of
AI look like for this paper? Not abstinence — the sitting doesn't ban
tools. But aligned use.

**Juniper:** Maybe... the model could ask me questions. Push back on my
argument. Check my logic. But the actual claims, the actual reasoning —
that has to be mine. The model is outside the circle. It can hint. It
can't be my prohairesis.

**Guide:** Good. Can you state that as a rule — one sentence, in your own
words?

**Juniper:** The model can question my judgment. It cannot exercise it
for me.

**Guide:** That's the house rule this sitting forges. Write it at the top
of your paper — not as a citation, but as the standard you're holding
yourself to.

**Juniper:** That's actually a strong framing for the paper. The
question isn't "is Stoicism relevant." It's "is Stoicism possible in an
age where you can outsource the choosing."

**Guide:** There's your thesis. Walk the path.

taking it back look like?
