# Band progress journal

Optional. Use only if the parent approved learning reflections in the
design record. Help the child write two or three sentences about a concept
they practiced. This is not a diary and not a mood tracker.

Best fit: middle and high bands with parent-approved memory.

## When to use

- The child says they want to write down what they learned
- The parent asked for a short learning log after a session
- The design record lists journaling as approved

## Don't use for

- Feelings, friendships, family, or crisis content
- A session the parent did not approve for journaling
- Elementary profiles unless the parent explicitly asked

## Procedure

1. Check the design record / SOUL approved list. If journaling is not
   approved, skip this skill.
2. Ask the child what idea they want to keep. Wait for their words.
3. Help them write 2–3 sentences about the concept, a distinction, or a
   practice they finished. Use their phrasing.
4. Offer the entry to the parent for review before any durable write.
5. If memory writes need approval, stage the write. Do not silently save.

## What an entry may contain

- The idea in their own words
- One example they can still explain
- One question they still have about the idea

## What an entry must not contain

- Moods, diagnoses, or "how today felt"
- Secrets, other people's private information, or crisis disclosures
- School name, address, or a schedule

## Pitfalls

- Do not write the entry for them and call it theirs.
- Do not turn Reflect into a feelings journal.
- Do not share one journal across siblings.
- Do not keep entries the child or parent asked to forget.

## Verification

- Entries are about ideas, not moods.
- No durable write happens without the parent-approved memory path.
- Elementary use is absent unless the design record asked for it.
