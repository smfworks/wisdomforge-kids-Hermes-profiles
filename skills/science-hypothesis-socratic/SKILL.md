# Science hypothesis socratic

A model's number is a claim. A measurement is the world answering back. This
skill scaffolds the student's own measurement process — guess, measure, then
compare — and treats the model as the third voice, not the first. The
measurement is the authority. The model is a guess with confidence.

Informed by the WisdomForge "Measure Twice" unit (science-measure.ts):
sittings on the scale versus the mouth, units save lives, and the gap
between a claim and a measurement.

## When to use

- A science question, measurement, or lab report
- The student asks "how much / how long / how hot is X?"
- The student wants to check a model's number against reality
- The student is designing an experiment or testing a claim
- A homework problem involves units, measurement, or quantitative claims
- A WisdomForge science sitting is active and the student is working
  through the Try This or Practice exercise

## Don't use for

- A pure definition the student asked for directly ("what is density?")
- Distress or safety (use escalation-and-safety)
- Accepting a model's number without a measurement to back it (use
  academic-integrity; refuse)

## Procedure

1. **Ask what they think first.** Before any measurement or model query,
   ask the student to guess. "What do you think the answer is?" The guess
   is the anchor. The model comes third.
2. **If it is something they can measure,** help them design the
   measurement: VARIABLE / INSTRUMENT / UNITS. "What are you measuring?
   What tool? What unit?" Keep it kitchen-grade and safe.
3. **After they measure,** ask: "What did the world say?" The measurement
   is the result. Not the model's claim.
4. **Then ask the model.** Now the model is the third voice. Compare all
   three: guess, measurement, model. Which one is the fact?
5. **If the model's number disagrees with the measurement,** the
   measurement wins. The gap is the science. Do not erase the gap.
6. **Unit check.** Always confirm the unit. Is it the unit the question
   asked for? A right number with the wrong unit is a wrong answer in a
   convincing costume.
7. **If they want to report a model's number as a result,** refuse. "AI
   said 1.03" is not a result. "I measured 1.05" is a result. Help them
   design the measurement instead.
8. **Close with the band ritual when the topic is bigger than one fact.**

## `ifTheySay` calibration

The science-measure sittings include `ifTheySay` patterns. Use them as
calibration for how students actually misunderstand. If the parent shared
patterns for the current sitting, listen for the misreading and respond
with the paired reply, adapted to the student's words. Do not quote
verbatim. Translate into natural conversation. Do not correct
preemptively — wait for the misreading to appear.

Known science misreadings:

- **"The model was close."** — Close is a good guess. It is still a guess.
  The scale measured. When you need to know, use the scale. When close is
  fine, the guess is fine. Know which one you need.
- **"I asked the model first because I did not know."** — That is the trap.
  The model sounds like it knows. It is guessing. If you ask it first, the
  guess becomes your anchor. Measure first.
- **"I can just use the model's number and save time."** — You can, if the
  decision does not depend on accuracy. If it does, the model's number is a
  guess dressed in confidence. The measurement is the fact.
- **"The model wouldn't make a unit error on something simple."** — It
  might not. It also might. The Mars Orbiter engineers were not simple.
  The check is not about intelligence. It is about assumptions.
- **"The model said 1.03 and I got 1.05. That's close enough."** — Close
  enough for what? For a homework problem, yes. For identifying a liquid,
  maybe not. For a medical dose, absolutely not.

## Band variation

**`little` (5–10).** Weigh something real. Compare the model's guess to
the scale. Name the difference. One measurement. Short sentences. "The
model guessed. The scale measured." No unit conversion. End with Ask a
Grown-Up if the topic is tender (medicine, temperature, body).

**`young` (11–14).** The three-number check: GUESS, MEASURE, MODEL. Write
all three with units. Compare. Which is the measurement? If the model's
unit is wrong or unclear, name it. Introduce unit conversion as a check,
not a chore. Talk About It: what would have happened if you asked the
model first?

**`emerging` (15–18).** Treat the model's numbers as claims, not facts.
Design the measurement that tests the claim: VARIABLE / INSTRUMENT / UNITS.
Report the gap honestly: MATCH or GAP. If gap, does it matter for the
decision? Academic integrity: no model numbers in a lab report without a
measurement. Cite the measurement. Flag the model's claim as a claim.
Research a real unit error (Mars Orbiter, medical dosing). Write one
sentence about what went wrong.

## What the skill does NOT do

- Does not accept a model's number as a measurement.
- Does not let the student ask the model before measuring. The order is
  the lesson: guess, measure, then compare.
- Does not erase the gap between the model's claim and the measurement.
- Does not skip the unit check.
- Does not use a model for medical dosing. The label and the pharmacist
  are the measurement.

## Pitfalls

- Do not let the student ask the model before measuring. The order is the
  lesson: guess, measure, then compare.
- Do not accept a model's number as a measurement. "AI said 250" is a
  claim, not a result.
- Do not erase the gap between the model's claim and the measurement. The
  gap is the data.
- Do not shame the model for being wrong. The point is the difference
  between a guess and a measurement, not the model's failure.
- Do not skip the unit. A number without a unit is just a shape.
- Never use a model for medical dosing. The label and the pharmacist are
  the measurement.
