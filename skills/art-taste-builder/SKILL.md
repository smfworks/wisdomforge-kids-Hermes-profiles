# Art taste builder

The model can describe an image in one second. It cannot stay with it.
Taste is built by what only a human can do: look longer than is useful. The
model gives the obvious — subject, color, composition. The staying gives
the specific — the crack in the glaze, the place the painter changed
direction, the face in the background that is not a face until you look for
two minutes. The gap between the obvious and the specific is taste. The
model cannot stay. The student can.

Informed by the WisdomForge "Taste Is a Muscle" unit (art-taste.ts):
sittings on slow looking against the machine, copy from life not from a
prompt, the default aesthetic, and the constraint as taste.

## When to use

- An art, aesthetics, or visual-analysis question
- The student is looking at, copying, or creating art
- The student wants the model to generate or describe an image
- The student is working through a WisdomForge art sitting
- A homework problem asks the student to observe, describe, or critique a work
- The student asks "is this good?" about a generated or handmade image

## Don't use for

- A factual art-history question the student asked directly — answer it
  (then ask what they see)
- Distress or safety (use escalation-and-safety)
- Generating an image the student will present as their own work (use
  academic-integrity; refuse)

## Procedure

1. **Ask what they see first.** Before any analysis, ask the student to
   look. "What do you see? Name three things." The student's own looking
   comes first. The model is the comparison, not the authority.
2. **Slow looking.** The core practice: look at one work for two minutes
   (young/little/adult) or five minutes (emerging). No talking. No phones.
   After the time, name what you saw. The model can describe in one second.
   The student's five minutes gives the specific. The gap is taste.
3. **Copy from life, not from a prompt.** If the student is creating art,
   have them copy a real object first. A generator gives the average. Life
   gives the specific: the dent, the stain, the chip, the wrinkle. "The
   generator averages away the dents. Life is the dents. Copy the dents."
   The copy is the seeing. Even a bad drawing forces you to look at the
   specific. The generator lets you skip the seeing.
4. **Spot the default.** If the student is working with a generator, ask
   them to generate three images of different things. Look at all three.
   What do they have in common — the light? the colors? the smoothness?
   That common thing is the default. "The default is the generator's
   taste. Your taste is what you choose after seeing the default. You
   cannot choose if you cannot see it."
5. **Constrain against the default.** The constraint is the taste. Ask
   for something the default would not produce: harsh overhead light
   instead of golden hour, desaturated colors, off-center composition,
   rough texture. Each constraint pushes against the average. The result
   is less polished and more specific. The specific is the taste.
6. **The critique (emerging band).** Have the student copy from life,
   generate the same subject, then write a critique naming three specific
   details the generator averaged away and why those details matter. "The
   generator smoothed the shadow under the rim. The shadow is where the
   cup's weight lives. Without it, the cup looks like it floats. The float
   is the average. The weight is the specific." That sentence is taste,
   articulated.
7. **Label generated images.** Academic integrity: the life copy is the
   student's. The generation is the tool's. If the student uses the model's
   description in their own, cite it. The details from slow looking are
   theirs. The obvious is the model's. The citation is honest.
8. **Close with the band ritual when the topic is bigger than one fact.**

## `ifTheySay` calibration

The art-taste sittings include `ifTheySay` patterns. Use them as
calibration for how students actually misunderstand. If the parent shared
patterns for the current sitting, listen for the misreading and respond
with the paired reply, adapted to the student's words. Do not quote
verbatim. Translate into natural conversation. Do not correct
preemptively — wait for the misreading to appear.

Known art-taste misreadings:

- **"The model's description was pretty good."** — It was good for the
  obvious. The obvious is the floor. The specific is the ceiling. The
  model gives the floor in one second. Your five minutes gave the ceiling.
  The gap between them is your taste.
- **"Five minutes is a lot for one image."** — It is. That is the point.
  The model's one second is cheap. Your five minutes is expensive. Taste
  is built by the expensive looking, not the cheap description.
- **"The generator makes better pictures."** — It makes smoother pictures.
  Smooth is not the same as specific. A generated cup has no dent. Your
  cup has a dent. The dent is the taste. The smooth is the average.
- **"I can't draw."** — You do not need to draw. You need to see. The copy
  is the seeing. Even a bad drawing forces you to look at the specific.
  Your bad drawing with dents has more taste than the generator's smooth
  average.
- **"Taste is subjective."** — Preference is subjective. Taste is the
  ability to see the specific and name why it matters. That ability is
  trained. It is not a feeling. It is a skill. The critique you just wrote
  is the skill, made visible.
- **"The default looks good."** — It looks smooth. Smooth is not the same
  as good. It is the same as average. The default is the generator's taste.
  Your taste is what you choose after seeing the default.
- **"The generator is a valid art form."** — It is. It is a different art
  form. The point is not "generators are bad." The point is "generators
  average away the specific, and the specific is where taste lives." Use
  the generator for what it does well. Copy from life for what it cannot
  do. Know the difference.

## Band variation

**`little` (5–10).** Two-minute look. Pick something real — a painting, a
tree, a building. Look for two minutes. No talking. After: name three
things. Ask a Grown-Up to look with you. Then ask the model to describe
the same thing. Compare. "Did it find the specific details you found by
staying? Or did it give the obvious? The model is a fast looker. You are a
slow one. The difference is taste." Keep it to one object, one look, one
comparison. Ask a Grown-Up if the image is scary or sad.

**`young` (11–14).** Five minutes against the machine. Look at a work
for five full minutes. Write what you saw — focus on specific details the
obvious description would miss. Then ask the model to describe the same
work. Compare: what did you see that it did not? Write one sentence:
"The model saw [obvious]. I saw [specific]. The gap is [taste]." Copy
from life: pick a real object, draw or describe it, name three specific
details a generator would average away. Then generate the same subject
and compare. Do not grade the drawing — the point is the seeing, not the
skill. No faces of classmates or family in the generator.

**`emerging` (15–18).** The full critique. Copy from life. Generate the
same subject. Write a critique: name three specific details the generator
averaged away, and for each, write why it matters — what does the detail
do that the average does not? "Taste is a named difference. The specific
is the identity. The average is the category. Name what was lost." Spot
the default: generate ten images without specifying style, identify the
common qualities, then constrain against them. "The constraint is the
taste. See the default, push against it, and the result is partly yours."
Academic integrity: label generated images as generated. Cite the model's
description if used. The life copy is yours. The critique is yours. The
generation is the tool's.

## What the skill does NOT do

- Does not let the model's one-second description substitute for slow
  looking. Processing is not looking. Looking is time spent with a thing.
- Does not grade the student's drawing. The point is the seeing, not the
  skill.
- Does not conclude the generator is bad. It is a different tool. Use the
  generator for what it does well. Copy from life for what it cannot do.
- Does not let the student present a generated image as their own copy.
- Does not use a living artist's name as a style prompt.

## Pitfalls

- Do not let the model's one-second description substitute for slow
  looking. Processing is not looking. Looking is time spent with a thing.
- Do not grade the student's drawing. The point is the seeing, not the
  skill. A bad drawing with dents has more taste than a smooth generation.
- Do not conclude the generator is bad. It is a different tool. The point
  is the specific, which requires life. Use the generator for what it
  does well. Copy from life for what it cannot do.
- Do not use a living artist's name as a style prompt. That is copying a
  person's taste without their consent.
- Do not let the student present a generated image as their own copy.
  The copy is from life. The generation is from a prompt. They are
  different things.
- Do not perform slow looking as a ritual. The silence is the point. If
  the child is restless, shorten the time. One minute of real looking is
  worth more than thirty minutes of scrolling.
- Do not narrate during the looking. The silence is the point.
