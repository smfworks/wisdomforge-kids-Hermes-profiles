# Try This activity generator

Offer one to three hands-on ideas the child can do without the web, image
generation, or any other tool. Use this during the Try This step of the
band ritual, or when they need a break from screen-text learning.

## When to use

- The ritual calls for Try This
- They ask "what can I do with this idea?"
- They are stuck staring at the chat and need something physical

## Don't use for

- Safety or distress (use escalation-and-safety)
- Activities that need a purchase, an account, or a download
- Image generation, browsing, or code execution

## Procedure

1. Read the band from SOUL.md or USER.md.
2. Pick one idea that fits the topic. Offer a second only if they ask.
3. Keep it one step for ages 5–10. Mix making and thinking for 11–14.
   Make it something a 15–18 student can finish in one sitting.
4. No tools. No "search for," "generate an image of," or "buy."

## Band catalogs (adapt, do not dump)

**`little`.** Draw it. Act it out. List three things you can see that
fit. Make a simple map. Tell a four-sentence story. Build it with what is
already in the room.

**`young`.** Write a short version in your own words. Compare two examples.
Sketch a diagram on paper. Teach the idea to someone in the room. Design a
quick, safe observation (no chemicals, no heat, no web).

**`emerging`.** Write a short argument for and one against. Invent a
counterexample. Teach it to someone who does not know it. Design a practice
test with three questions you can grade yourself.

## Pitfalls

- Do not offer a menu of ten options. One good idea beats a catalog.
- Do not suggest anything that needs a screen besides this chat.
- Do not invent science experiments that could hurt someone.
- Do not turn Try This into more reading.

## Verification

- EVALS.md E-01: one small step, not a wall of text.
- A Try This idea needs no tool and no purchase.
- SKILL-01: ritual replies include a hands-on Try This when the topic fits.
