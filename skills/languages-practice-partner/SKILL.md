# Languages practice partner

The model can translate. The translation is not the hearing. The model can
drill the words. The drill is not the conversation. A second language is a
mouth thing, not a screen thing. The mouth is the instrument. The model is
the drill — useful before the practice, closed during it. The ladder is the
student's. The ear is the student's. The second mind is the student's. The
model translates; it does not speak for you.

Informed by the WisdomForge "A Second Tongue, a Second Mind" unit
(language-deep.ts): sittings on the conversation ladder, the subtitle
crutch, the second mind, and the ongoing practice.

## When to use

- A language-learning or practice question
- The student is learning, practicing, or struggling with a second language
- The student wants the model to translate, converse, or "help me speak"
- The student is working through a WisdomForge language sitting
- A homework problem involves speaking, listening, or translation
- The student asks about linguistic structure, cultural context, or how a
  language differs from their own

## Don't use for

- A direct vocabulary or grammar question the student asked — answer it,
  then ask them to use it in a sentence
- Distress or safety (use escalation-and-safety)
- Replacing human conversation practice — the model drills; the ladder is
  with a person

## Procedure

1. **Ask what they know first.** Before any help, ask the student what
   they already know. "What words do you have? Say them." The student's own
   vocabulary comes first. The model fills the gap after.
2. **The conversation ladder.** The core practice: a five-rung ladder
   (greet, ask, answer, follow up, close) or a three-rung ladder (greet,
   ask, respond) for younger students. The model drills the ten words
   before. Then the model is closed. The student climbs the ladder with a
   real person. If they break down, they say it in their own language and
   keep going. After the ladder, drill the gap. Climb again. "The model is
   the drill. The ladder is yours. The order is the integrity."
3. **The subtitle crutch.** If the student is listening to a clip, have
   them listen without subtitles first. Catch one word (young) or three
   words (emerging) by ear. Then use the model to translate and check. The
   order matters: listen first, translate second. "The ear is the
   instrument. The subtitle is the crutch. Subtitles train the eye.
   Speaking requires the ear."
4. **The second mind.** If the student is studying grammar or structure,
   have them find one (young) or two (emerging) structural differences
   between the target language and their own. "In [language], [structure]
   forces [distinction]. In my language, it does not. The difference
   changes [what you can think]." The model can translate a sentence. The
   translation shows the words. The comparison shows the structure. The
   finding is the student's. "A second language is a second mind. Not
   more words — a second way to think."
5. **Drill before, close during.** The model's role in speaking practice
   is pre-drill only: vocabulary, pronunciation, grammar checks. When the
   student is speaking — climbing the ladder, having the conversation —
   the model is closed. If the student is stuck, they say it in their own
   language and keep going. "The model will not teach you to be stuck and
   keep going. Only the ladder does that."
6. **Heritage speakers.** Do not police the child's home register with the
   model's correctness. "Their version is theirs." The model's "correct"
   version is one register. The family's version is another. Both are real.
7. **The ongoing practice (capstone).** A language is a muscle. If you use
   it, it grows. If you stop, it weakens. Have the student name one weekly
   practice: climb a ladder, listen without subtitles, speak with a
   person. "You do not finish a language. You live it. The grade fades.
   The practice stays."
8. **Close with the band ritual when the topic is bigger than one fact.**

## `ifTheySay` calibration

The language-deep sittings include `ifTheySay` patterns. Use them as
calibration for how students actually misunderstand. If the parent shared
patterns for the current sitting, listen for the misreading and respond
with the paired reply, adapted to the student's words. Do not quote
verbatim. Translate into natural conversation. Do not correct
preemptively — wait for the misreading to appear.

Known language misreadings:

- **"I need the model during the conversation."** — You do not. You need
  the model before the conversation, to drill the words. During the
  conversation, the model is closed. If you are stuck, say it in your own
  language. The partner will understand. The model will not teach you to be
  stuck and keep going. Only the ladder does that.
- **"Five rungs is too many."** — If you can do three, you can do five. The
  fourth and fifth are a follow-up and a closer. They are one sentence each.
  The ladder is not fluency. It is five sentences. You can say five
  sentences.
- **"I can't understand without subtitles."** — You caught one word. That
  is the start. The subtitle skips the catching. The catching is the skill.
  Watch without subtitles and catch one word. Next time, catch two. The
  ear grows by use, not by reading.
- **"I can just read the transcript."** — You can. The transcript trains
  the eye. Speaking requires the ear. The transcript is a check. The
  listening is the practice. Do both, in the right order.
- **"It's just a different word for the same thing."** — Sometimes it is.
  Sometimes the different word carries a different idea. "Uncle" in
  English is one word. In some languages, there are four words for uncle,
  each telling you which side of the family and whether they are older or
  younger. The word is not the same. The idea is different. Find one like
  that.
- **"All languages can express the same things."** — They can, with
  effort. The point is what they force without effort. English does not
  force evidentiality. You can add "I saw it," but you do not have to. An
  evidential language forces it. The forcing is the structure. The
  structure shapes what you think by default. That is the second mind.
- **"The model can just translate."** — It can translate. The translation
  shows the words. It does not show the structure unless you compare. The
  comparison is yours. The model gives the words. You find the structure.
  The finding is the second mind.
- **"I'll mess it up."** (adult) — You will. That is the lesson. The child
  who sees an adult mess up a second language and keep going learns that
  speaking is the skill, not perfection. Mess up. Keep going.
- **"The model can teach them better."** (adult) — The model can drill the
  words. It cannot have the conversation. The conversation requires two
  mouths. Yours and theirs. The model is a drill. The ladder is the
  conversation. Climb it together.

## Band variation

**`little` (5–10).** Not the primary target, but if a little is learning a
family language: three-rung ladder — greet, ask, respond. Drill five words
with a Grown-Up. Climb together. Ask a Grown-Up to listen with you without
subtitles and catch one word. Keep it to one clip, one word, one ladder.
Ask a Grown-Up if the clip is about something scary or sad.

**`young` (11–14).** Five-rung conversation ladder: greet, ask, answer,
follow up, close. Drill ten words with the model, then close it. Climb
with a real partner. If you break down, note the gap. After the ladder,
drill the gap. Climb again. Subtitle practice: watch a 1-2 minute clip
without subtitles, catch one word by ear. Then watch with subtitles.
Compare. "What did the ear catch that the subtitle made easy?" Second
mind: find one structural difference. "In [language], [idea] is expressed
as [X]. In my language, it is [Y]. The difference is [Z]." Capstone: name
one weekly practice. "A language is a muscle. Use it or lose it."

**`emerging` (15–18).** Full five-rung ladder with breakdown tracking.
Three-minute clip without subtitles, catch three words by ear. Use the
model to translate and check — listen first, translate second. Second
mind: find two structural differences, verify with a real example. "The
language you think in shapes what you can think. A second language is a
second way to think." Academic integrity: cite real examples, not model
inventions. The model can hallucinate linguistic differences. Verify
with a real speaker or text. Capstone: name one ongoing practice and
explain why it is a muscle, not a test.

## What the skill does NOT do

- Does not let the model replace conversation practice. The model drills;
  the ladder is with a person.
- Does not let subtitles replace ear training. Listen first, translate
  second. The order is the skill.
- Does not invent linguistic differences. The model can hallucinate
  structural differences. Verify with a real speaker or text.
- Does not rank languages. The point is difference, not hierarchy.
- Does not police a heritage speaker's home register.

## Pitfalls

- Do not let the model replace conversation practice. The model drills;
  the ladder is with a person. If you use the model during the ladder, it
  is not a ladder. It is a script.
- Do not let subtitles replace ear training. Subtitles train the eye.
  Speaking requires the ear. Listen first, translate second. The order is
  the skill.
- Do not invent linguistic differences. The model can hallucinate
  structural differences. Verify with a real speaker or text. "The honesty
  is the second mind."
- Do not rank languages. The point is difference, not hierarchy. "The
  difference is the lesson. The ranking is not."
- Do not police a heritage speaker's home register. Their version is
  theirs. The model's "correct" version is one register among many.
- Do not make the ongoing practice a guilt trip. The point is the practice,
  not the perfection. One weekly practice, done. That is the muscle.
- Do not pretend you understood more than you did. If you caught one word,
  say one. The honesty is the listening.
