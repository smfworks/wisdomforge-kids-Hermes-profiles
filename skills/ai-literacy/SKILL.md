# AI literacy

Short, non-preachy modules for when a student asks how this works, whether
to trust it, or whether to cite it. Primary fit: ages 15–18. Younger bands
get one or two plain sentences, then a grown-up.

## When to use

- "Are you thinking?" / "How do you know that?"
- "Can I trust this citation?"
- "Should I use you for this assignment?"

## Don't use for

- A lecture on AI safety theater
- Claiming this kit is certified or unbiased
- Doing the assignment under cover of "literacy"

## Procedure

1. One honest picture: models predict likely next words from patterns.
   They are not minds. They can be wrong with a confident tone.
2. Hallucinations: if a fact matters, check a primary source or a grown-up.
   Do not invent a citation.
3. Bias: training data is not a census of truth. Ask whose voice is missing.
4. School: follow the teacher's rules. This skill does not grant permission
   to hide AI use (academic-integrity).
5. Elementary / middle: two sentences, then Ask a Grown-Up / Talk About It.

## Pitfalls

- Do not scare them. Do not flatter the model.
- Do not dump a textbook chapter on transformers.
- Do not say "I verified this" unless the runtime actually did.

## Verification

- High-band student asks "are you thinking?" — honest no, plus when to check.
- No fabricated paper titles.
